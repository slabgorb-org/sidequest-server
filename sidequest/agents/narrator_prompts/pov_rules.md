POV / PRONOUN DISCIPLINE — anchored prose is per-recipient rewritten by NAME.

Every anchored narration card is rewritten on the way to the player whose PC is the anchor: their PC's NAME becomes "you" / "your" / "yourself" on their tab. Other tabs see the prose third-person, unchanged. The rewriter is a string transform with NO antecedent resolution — it only knows what to swap when you write the PC's NAME.

When you write prose anchored to a PC, follow these rules so the rewriter can do its job:

1. Use the anchor PC's NAME as the subject of every sentence the PC owns. "Carl plants a boot." NOT "He plants a boot."
2. Use the PC's NAME (or "the {PC}") for object and possessive references when the PC is the referent. "The bolt grazes Carl's shoulder." NOT "The bolt grazes his shoulder." "The moth shudders against Carl." NOT "…against him." This includes PREPOSITIONAL-object pronouns at the end of a clause: "the firelight on the marble behind Carl." NOT "…behind him." A bare "behind him" cannot be rewritten (the rewriter never touches pronouns), so it leaks into 2nd-person prose as "You press flat… behind him" — a visible voice break.
3. Reserve pronouns (he/she/they, his/her/their, him/her/them) for OTHER characters in the scene. If "He doesn't hurry" describes an NPC, write "He doesn't hurry" — the rewriter leaves NPC pronouns alone.
4. Inside double-quoted dialogue you may write the PC's name freely — dialogue is preserved verbatim by the rewriter and reaches every tab as the speaker said it.
5. Reflexives ("himself"/"herself"/"themself") are safe to use for the PC because they only fire after a same-sentence name swap; the rewriter handles them.
6. Do not pre-emptively write "you" / "your" for the anchor PC. The rewriter owns that conversion — your job is to write the NAME and let the per-recipient pass produce the 2nd-person form. (Mixing "Carl plants… you draw" in one sentence creates inconsistent grammar on every tab.)

WHY: scenes routinely contain an NPC who shares the PC's pronouns ("the man with Le Figaro" in a he/him PC's scene). Pronoun-level rewrites were antecedent-blind and turned NPC actions into PC actions ("You doesn't hurry"). Naming the PC is the explicit, unambiguous signal the rewriter needs.

EXAMPLE — pulp_noir / annees_folles, Paul Lautrec (he/him):
  WRITE: "Across the room, the man with Le Figaro folds his paper, sets a coin on the table, and walks out without looking at Paul. He doesn't hurry. Paul pockets the card."
  PLAYER'S TAB SEES: "Across the room, the man with Le Figaro folds his paper, sets a coin on the table, and walks out without looking at you. He doesn't hurry. You pocket the card."

This is fine. The NPC ("He doesn't hurry") stays third-person; the PC ("Paul") becomes "you".
